The game changer

The old system was a screener. The new system is a trading engine. It finds the setup, prices the trade, determines the structure, sizes the position, and places the order. Your job is to review what happened — not to make decisions.

What the seven pillars actually measure

Think of it like a jury. You need a minimum of four independent jurors to agree before a verdict. Each looks at completely different evidence. When they all point the same way, the signal is real.

P1

SCALABILITYCan we actually trade this?

Is this ticker liquid enough for options on a $50k account? MU, NVDA, ORCL score 1.0. A tiny biotech with no options volume scores 0. This is a gate — if it's 0, nothing else matters.

P2

OPTIONS FLOWIs smart money making a directional bet?

Unusual Whales tracks large options sweeps across all exchanges. We check whether the sweep is call-dominant or put-dominant AND whether Unusual Whales has classified the rows as "bullish" or "bearish." Raw volume without classification does not vote on direction — that was the bug that made everything look LONG.

P3

THREE SIGNALSCongress, Gov contracts, Social velocity

P3a Congressional: A US Representative bought $15k+ of stock disclosed under the STOCK Act. Committee members weighted higher. P3b Gov Contract: News with DoD/DARPA/MDA/Pentagon anchor keywords — not just "approval." P3c Social velocity: 24h mentions 150%+ above the 7-day average — organic surge, not noise.

P4

META-CONVERGENCEThe boost

When 4+ independent pillars fire simultaneously, we add +1.5 to the composite score. Independent sources converging is worth more than the sum of their parts — that's what separates a convergence trade from a trend follow.

P6

CATALYSTThe event that forces resolution

Without a binary catalyst, there is no super signal — period. Earnings, FDA drug approval dates (PDUFA), and FOMC meetings create a moment where price must move. Options premium inflates into the event. If positioned correctly beforehand, you capture a large move for a defined, limited cost. Sweet spot is 20–40 days away.

P7

QUANT MOMENTUMDo the numbers agree?

Institutional flow polarity (FinBERT-scored STOCK Act filings) aligned with 5-day price trend. Both bullish → 1.0 LONG. Both bearish → 1.0 SHORT. Conflicting → 0.5. The quantitative confirmation layer.

Why SHORT and not LONG?

P7 shows institutional flow polarity −0.500 — STOCK Act filings show net sellers in MU. P2 is mixed (11 bullish / 9 bearish) so it doesn't vote strongly either way. SHORT wins. Price being up +21% over 5 days is the conflict — institutions selling into strength is a classic distribution pattern. The system correctly flags this SHORT with partial P7 score.

Why a Bear Put Spread and not just buying puts?

A bear put spread is a defined-risk, defined-reward structure. You pay $1,129 to enter — that is your maximum possible loss, it cannot go higher. Maximum gain is ~$871 if MU closes below $950 at expiry. A naked put costs more upfront and if MU doesn't move you lose 100% of premium to time decay. The spread limits both risk and reward, but the R/R is acceptable when hit rate is above 50%.

Exactly what happens, step by step

The precise sequence from signal development to trade logged. Nothing requires human involvement except initial setup.

1

EVERY 5 MINData ingestion

10 adapters run in parallel. ApeWisdom pulls Reddit mentions. Unusual Whales pulls options flow. Quiver pulls congressional filings. Finnhub pulls news. Polygon pulls prices. ~73,000 posts per day flow into TimescaleDB on the Windows Server.

2

EVERY 15 MINSentiment scoring

FinBERT (a financial language model) reads every new post and assigns a polarity score from −1.0 (very bearish) to +1.0 (very bullish). Congressional filings become institutional flow polarity. Social posts become retail sentiment. These feed P7 and P3c.

3

EVERY 15 MINConvergence scan — 1,025 tickers

The engine scores all active tickers across 7 pillars in ~90 seconds. For each ticker it reads from 6 database tables: options_flow, institutional_positions, sentiment_scores, price_snapshots, earnings_calendar, pdufa_calendar. Results written to the convergence_signals table.

4

ON DETECTIONStructure plan auto-attached

When score ≥ 4.0, 4+ pillars, catalyst present: the structure recommender fires immediately. Reads current price, computes 30-day HV, runs Black-Scholes, picks structure (spread or strangle), selects strikes and expiry, stores the full trade plan in the database alongside the signal. The dashboard always shows the structure — no manual step.

5

9:30–4:00 ETExecution check

After every convergence scan during market hours: Is macro RISK_ON? Signal fresh (<20 min)? Already traded this ticker today? Enough cash? All guards pass → constructs OCC symbol (e.g. MU 260717P00970000), calculates limit price at estimate +10%, submits order to Alpaca paper API.

6

IMMEDIATELYOrder logged and dashboard updated

Every placed order written to options_trade_log with the Alpaca order ID, structure type, estimated debit, and signal context. Dashboard Convergence view reflects current state within minutes.

Questions you probably have

  • Why options and not just buying stock?
    Three reasons. Leverage: a $1,129 spread on MU gives exposure to a $95,000 notional position (100 shares × ~$950). Defined risk: you cannot lose more than the premium paid — the position cannot go against you beyond $1,129. Catalyst asymmetry: options premium inflates into binary events then collapses after. If positioned correctly beforehand, you capture a large move for a small, defined cost. You cannot replicate that payoff by buying stock.
  • What if the price estimate is way off?
    The system labels all prices as estimated — verify against broker. Black-Scholes on HV30 is directionally correct but numerically off, especially pre-earnings when IV inflates. The limit order is placed at estimate +10% buffer. Sprint 12 replaces this with live IV data. For now, check thinkorswim or the Alpaca chain for the actual mid-price before going live.
  • What if macro goes RISK_OFF?
    The system completely stops executing new orders when macro returns RISK_OFF. The convergence scan still runs and finds signals — it just doesn't execute them. When macro returns to RISK_ON or NEUTRAL, execution resumes automatically on the next cycle. Current macro state: NEUTRAL +0.147 — execution is live.
  • How does the system decide how many contracts to buy?
    Target risk = equity × 2%. On $50k that's $1,000. Contracts = floor($1,000 / max_loss_per_contract), capped at 5. For MU at $1,129 max loss: floor($1,000 / $1,129) = 0, rounds up to 1 contract. When the account reaches ~$113k, 2 contracts become appropriate. Sizing scales automatically with equity — no manual adjustment.
  • When do we close positions?
    Phase 1 right now: Manual close. Check the dashboard daily. When the bear put spread is worth 50% more than you paid — close it. For MU at $1,129 debit, close when worth ~$1,564 (a $435 gain). Also close at 100% loss to preserve capital. Sprint 12: The system monitors open positions every cycle and submits closing orders automatically when the target is hit.
  • What does "the system learns from past trades" mean?
    The options_trade_log records every trade with signal context — which pillars fired, composite score, catalyst type — and its P&L when closed. Over time the system can calculate: "when P6 fires with earnings 20–30 days away, what is the win rate?" or "do signals with congressional buy outperform?" This data allows pillar weights to be tuned based on evidence. The system improves as it accumulates trade history.
